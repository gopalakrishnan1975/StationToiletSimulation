# How AI Changes the Way We Make Policy — A Railway Toilet Story

**Audience:** senior railway management. **Format:** a 40-minute live teaching case, not a 40-slide monologue.

## Opening (2 min)
A traveller spends 45 minutes reaching the station and another 30 minutes waiting, then boards a train without accessing a satisfactory WC. Who owns this failure—commercial, engineering, mechanical, medical, finance, or none?

## Act I: The surface problem (7 min)
1. On a 600 m platform, should we build one large block or three distributed blocks?
2. Female users spend more time per WC; change 12 to 18 female WCs. Watch queues and walking.
3. Ask why an unused or filthy toilet is a worse policy outcome than a toilet with a positive construction report.

## Act II: The invisible infrastructure (7 min)
4. A toilet needs GLR, OHT, pumping and electrical redundancy. It also creates wastewater: STP, equalization and sludge handling.
5. Clean toilets need cleaning frequency, trained staff, relief shifts and monitoring. Change footfall and water autonomy live.

## Act III: The economic surprise (9 min)
6. A ₹5 WC fee may improve accounting receipts but deter use; coach-cleanliness and passenger-welfare effects are externalities. Fees paid are transfers and should not be counted as newly created social surplus.
7. Challenge participants: if sanitation costs roughly ₹2.2 per boarding at a busy example station, why not include clean basic toilets in the ticket's service entitlement? Explain that **reallocating existing fare receipts** is different from **raising fares**.
8. Try an incremental fare, earmarked allocation and nonfare budget, then compare finance and welfare across free, charged and free-basic-plus-premium policies.

## Act IV: Governing with a living toolkit (10 min)
9. An LLM helped connect timetabling, queues, hydrology, facilities management, welfare economics and public finance. The output is not automatic truth; it is an inspectable, corrigible system of hypotheses.
10. Senior management chooses service guarantees, quality KPIs, ownership, budgets, and a controlled pilot. Publish assumptions and update them as evidence arrives.

## Closing (5 min)
Ask: *Which other policy has an invisible end-to-end system?* Examples: station access, level crossing closure, siding turnaround, rolling stock cleaning.

**Final message:** AI's highest value in administration is not better drafting; it lowers the cost of turning a policy intuition into a measurable, executable and revisable decision model.
